Diabetes Care

The 3-month review plan

Diabetes is controlled in cycles of three months — because HbA1c, the key report, itself reflects your average sugar of the last 3 months. Dr. Jain's care is built around that rhythm: measure honestly, adjust medicines conservatively, and review on time, every time.

🩸

HbA1c — the report that matters

HbA1c shows your 3-month average blood sugar. One fasting reading can mislead; HbA1c cannot. It is checked at every review so treatment decisions rest on a full quarter of data, not a single day.

💊

Conservative drug therapy

Patients value that medicines here are increased only when the reports truly demand it — the lowest effective therapy first, reviewed against sugar and BP together, with side-effects watched closely.

❤️

Sugar + BP, controlled together

Diabetes and blood pressure travel together and multiply each other's risk. Every review tracks both, so long-term control protects the heart, kidneys and eyes — not just the sugar number.

1

Baseline consultation

Bring all previous reports and current medicines. Sugar profile, BP and HbA1c are recorded, and your personal targets are set.

2

Settle into the plan

Medicines, diet and daily routine are adjusted in small, sustainable steps — nothing drastic that you cannot maintain.

3

3-month review

Fresh HbA1c and BP check against your targets. If control is good, the same therapy continues; if not, it is fine-tuned — and the next review is fixed before you leave.

4

Repeat, steadily

Regular 3-month reviews are what turn short-term control into long-term protection. Book each next review right from this page.

Everyday Guidance

Diabetic diet guide — the simple rules

General information to discuss at your consultation — not a substitute for the personalised diet plan the doctor gives you.

🍽️

The plate method

  • Half the plate: sabzi & salad
  • One quarter: dal / paneer / egg / chicken
  • One quarter: roti or rice — measured, not heaped
  • Eat slowly; stop at 80% full
✅

Choose more often

  • Lauki, torai, bhindi, palak, methi
  • Whole dals, chana, rajma, sprouts
  • Atta rotis, brown rice in small portions
  • Chaas, nimbu pani (no sugar)
⚠️

Keep rare & small

  • Mithai, cold drinks, packaged juices
  • White rice + aloo in the same meal
  • Fried snacks — samosa, pakode, namkeen
  • Late heavy dinners; eat by 8–8:30 pm
🚶

The 10-minute rule

A gentle 10–15 minute walk after meals blunts the sugar spike better than any single food rule. Small, daily, non-negotiable.

📒

Keep a sugar diary

Note fasting and post-meal readings on the days the doctor asks. Patterns in the diary make your 3-month review far more useful.

🧂

Mind the salt, too

With BP in the picture, go easy on papad, pickle, chips and extra table salt. Taste adjusts within a few weeks — sugar and BP both thank you.
